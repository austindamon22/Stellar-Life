What the models simplify

  • Mass cutoffs (~0.5, ~2, ~8, ~25 M☉) are approximate and shift with metallicity, rotation, mass loss and binary partners.
  • Star interiors follow the standard picture, not a full stellar-evolution code. The schematic view stretches layers so all are visible; true scale uses order-of-magnitude real sizes. In the massive-star onion, the shell radii (silicon, oxygen, neon, carbon, helium) are factor-of-two estimates from 15–25 M☉ presupernova models (Woosley, Heger & Weaver 2002; Sukhbold et al. 2016).
  • H-R tracks come from scaling relations shaped to match published tracks.
  • Interior time dilation assumes uniform density; the falling clock's far view ignores light-travel delay.
  • Star clusters use solar composition; real globular clusters such as M92 are metal-poor, so their turnoff stars are hotter and a little less massive than the model's.
  • The black hole camera shows a non-spinning hole with a thin disk and maps disk temperature to visible colours.
  • The Milky Way model has no dust lanes, and its arm positions are approximate.
  • The galaxy collision uses test-particle stars and no gas.
